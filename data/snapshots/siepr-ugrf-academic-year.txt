Applications for 2026 Autumn quarter are now open. APPLY NOW
The SIEPR Undergraduate Research Fellows (UGRF) program offers faculty-mentored research opportunities for Stanford undergraduates during the academic year. The program focuses on enriching the student research experience and offers additional opportunities for students to participate in the intellectual life of SIEPR.&nbsp;
Program Structure&nbsp;
Each quarter students and their faculty mentor will meet prior to the start of the quarter to agree on project goals and learning outcomes for a quarter-long research experience.
The Autumn Quarter program runs from September 22 to December 4, 2026.
Students and faculty should meet prior to the start of the quarter to agree on project goals and learning outcomes for a quarter-long research experience.
UGRFs will have the opportunity to participate in SIEPR policy forums and Summit.
Students will be paid via payroll for up to 15 hours a week.
Eligibility
UGRFs must be enrolled as full-time undergraduate students at Stanford. Coterm students who are interested in the program will need to hold undergraduate standing to be eligible for the SIEPR UGRF program.
Stanford students on hourly pay may not exceed 15 hours per week for all campus employment. Students with other hourly-paid commitments on campus may have limited hours of SIEPR-funded research. Time commitment should be discussed with faculty mentors prior to accepting the UGRF offer.
How to Apply
To apply for the SIEPR UGRF program, fill out the online application using the link below. Please follow the instructions in the application portal. The application will ask you to answer a few general questions about your academic interests.
Please prepare to upload these listed materials
A list of the current courses that you are enrolled in for the upcoming quarter.
Resume
A cover letter that addresses the following: Why are you interested in a SIEPR UGRF position?
What is your previous experience, if any, with research?
What are your personal research interests?
Questions?
If you have questions, please email&nbsp; siepr-fellowships@stanford.edu .

Building Historical Manufacturing Data with Multimodal AI
Open position
Mentor: Rebekah Dix&nbsp;
Positions available: 1&nbsp;
Project Description
The Thomas Register of American Manufacturers, published annually from 1905 to 2006, records firms by product and location and includes advertisements describing firms, brands, and capabilities. We will use multiple multimodal large language models to turn these complex scanned pages into structured data for studying how American industry changed over time. The fellow will help extract directory listings and advertisements, match firm names across entries, and build a hand-checked test dataset. We will use that dataset to compare open-source or open-weight models with proprietary models on accuracy, consistency, speed, and cost. The project will also explore linking the extracted firms to historical patent and copyright records and historical Census of Manufactures data.
RF Responsibilities
OCR and review directory listings and advertisements while preserving the original visible text
Extract and verify firm names, locations, product headings, advertisement content, and source-page references
Correct OCR errors caused by columns, headings, continuation text, and pages that mix directory listings with advertisements
Match firms across directory entries and advertisements using firm names, locations, and product context
Build a hand-checked test dataset covering varied layouts and scan quality, including verified OCR fields, firm-match labels, source pointers, and uncertainty flags
Run the same test material through multiple multimodal models, including open-source or open-weight and proprietary models, recording model version, prompts, settings, and output for each run
Compare models on OCR and structured-extraction accuracy, firm-matching quality, consistency, speed, and cost; record recurring errors and identify which page types require stronger models or manual review
Help explore links between the extracted firms and other historical sources, including patent and copyright records and Census of Manufactures data, documenting uncertain matches and differences across sources
Maintain clear error logs, matching notes, model-comparison results, and documentation
Qualifications
We are looking for a careful and curious student who enjoys working with messy historical data. The most important qualifications are attention to detail, reliability, and a willingness to document decisions clearly. Applicants should be comfortable working with spreadsheets and learning new tools. Experience with Python, OCR, large language models, record linkage, or archival research is useful but not required.

The Effects of Brazilian Vocational Education on Students
Open position
Mentor: Eric Bettinger&nbsp;
Positions available: 2
Project Description
Brazil reformed their vocational education in the last few years, and vocational education has expanded exponentially. We examine the reforms in Pernambuco and São Paulo. Using anonymous data, we quantitatively measure the impacts of these reforms, using matched comparisons and quasi-experimental methods as needed. We are also conducting qualitative interviews with stakeholders. This is a portfolio of projects ranging from examining optimal placement of specific vocational programs, to how it affects students' peer groups, to how it allows students to work during and after school.
RF Responsibilities
A successful candidate would contribute to either (or both) areas of inquiry. On our quantitative projects, we need someone who can manage data in Stata or R and conduct basic analysis (typically computing descriptive statistics and running basic regressions). On the qualitative projects, we need someone who can organize and build interview protocols and basic surveys. We are looking for people who can not only contribute to the tasks but also help us consider design and impact.
Qualifications
For quantitative analysis applicants: basic data skills and some familiarity with regression analysis
For qualitative analysis applicants: basic understanding of interviews, ethnographies, surveys, and/or focus groups
Above all, good organizational skills to help us process existing notes and transcripts
Portuguese is not required but a plus
Progress and Inequality: History, Science, and AI
Open position
Mentor: Lukas Althoff&nbsp;
Positions available: 2&nbsp;
Project Description
Our lab studies long-run economic change, with a focus on progress and inequality: how AI and earlier information technologies like the early internet reshape scientific discovery (see, e.g., anatomyofscience.com), and how historical institutions, from Jim Crow to the GI Bill, shape economic opportunity across generations.
Undergraduate Research Fellows join as junior collaborators on scoped pieces of this work — building and cleaning datasets, writing analysis code, reviewing literatures, and contributing to papers and public research products. Prior coursework in economics is not required; curiosity, care with data, and willingness to go deep matter more. Mentorship is close, and strong work can grow into a longer research relationship beyond the quarter.
RF Responsibilities
Own a well-defined workstream (for example, a dataset module, coding task, or literature review) and become the local expert on that piece
Clean, organize, and document data from historical archives and/or modern sources; write reproducible analysis code (typically Python or Stata)
Produce short research memos summarizing what you tried, what you found, and what is still uncertain
Use AI coding tools thoughtfully for analysis and documentation — as a way to move faster while still checking and understanding the results
Join regular check-ins, keep deadlines, and communicate blockers early so the project stays on track within a 10–15 hour/week schedule
Qualifications
Stanford undergraduate interested in economics, economic history, science and innovation, computer science, statistics, mathematics, or a related field
Comfortable (or eager to become comfortable) with quantitative work and careful data handling; prior coding experience in Python, R, or Stata is a plus but not required
Able to focus deeply, work independently for stretches of time, and follow through on open-ended tasks without constant supervision
Curious about both technology (AI, scientific progress) and history (institutions, inequality) — or excited to learn the side that is newer to you
Clear written communication and reliability; experience with AI tools (e.g., Claude, Codex, Copilot) is welcome when paired with independent judgment
Available for up to 15 hours per week during Autumn Quarter 2026
What (Actually) Works in US Jails? Evaluating the Expansion of IGNITE
Open position
Mentor: Marcella Alsan&nbsp;
Positions available: 2
Project Description
This project evaluates the expansion of the IGNITE (Inmate Growth Naturally and Intentionally Through Education) program across 40 jails nationwide. The study examines how the jail-based education program is implemented across different sites and assesses its impact on outcomes such as in-jail misconduct, rehabilitation, and successful community reentry after release.
RF Responsibilities
The Research Fellow will support a range of research and project activities. Primary responsibilities will include collecting and organizing publicly available social media data, conducting AI-assisted coding and classification of social media content, and assisting with event-study activities. Additional responsibilities may include data organization, documentation, and other administrative tasks.
Qualifications
Currently enrolled Stanford undergraduate (Economics or related major); strong attention to detail; strong data management and organizational skills; familiarity with coding or content analysis; understanding of social media platforms (especially Facebook); and ability to work independently and communicate effectively with project team members.
Digitizing Historical Drug Indications
Open position
Mentor: Rebekah Dix&nbsp;
Positions available: 1
Project Description
This project will digitize historical Physicians' Desk Reference (PDR) and American Hospital Formulary Service (AHFS) volumes, which document approved drug indications and broader therapeutic uses, including off-label uses. The research fellow will help create a human-validated test dataset from selected pages and compare open-source and proprietary OCR models. This work will identify an accurate and cost-effective approach for processing the larger collection. The resulting data will ultimately contribute to a historical drug-year panel of on- and off-label indications used to study how these forms of research affect future pharmaceutical innovation.
RF Responsibilities
Prepare accurate, human-validated transcriptions of selected PDR and AHFS pages
Run standardized comparisons of open-source and proprietary OCR models
Review model outputs and summarize accuracy and cost tradeoffs
Organize the test dataset and document the process clearly
Qualifications
Careful, organized, and attentive to detail
Comfortable checking digitized text against scanned documents
Basic coding or data experience preferred
Modernizing Governance
Open position
Mentor: Daniel E. Ho&nbsp;
Positions available: 2
Project Description
The Stanford Regulation, Evaluation, and Governance Lab (RegLab) is a social impact lab that partners with federal, state, and local government agencies to leverage AI and data science to modernize government and enable it to serve the public more effectively and equitably. Our work includes the development of AI and data science to improve tax administration and benefits adjudication, strengthen environmental regulation, and solve intractable problems faced each day by state and local governments.
RF Responsibilities
RFs will work with an interdisciplinary research team using AI and data science to address some of society's greatest challenges. Responsibilities will include data collection, management, and analysis; assistance with literature reviews and writing results; attendance and active participation in lab and team meetings; and support for other research tasks as needed.
Qualifications
Students with an interest in public sector technology, data science, public health, or environmental protection are particularly welcome to apply. We are seeking RFs who are intellectually curious, self-guided, and highly organized. Strong quantitative skills preferred.
AI &amp; Tax Policy
Open position
Mentor: Rebecca Lester&nbsp;
Positions available: 1
Project Description
How will AI impact U.S. fiscal policy? This project will study three ways that AI will impact tax policy: (1) changing tax collections due to the AI shock, (2) evaluating changes in tax authority enforcement and taxpayer compliance, and (3) altering the use of tax incentives for the location of AI companies and data centers. From this, the project will evaluate current proposals on whether and how to tax AI.
RF Responsibilities
Help assemble information, articles, and publicly available data from both government websites and public disclosures of AI hyperscalers. Working directly with the faculty member, we will prepare research articles and presentations. Creativity and persistence are two critical characteristics for the applicant.
Qualifications
Background in economics or related fields helpful, as is use of AI tools. Interest in AI-related policy issues is a must. No knowledge of taxation is needed.
