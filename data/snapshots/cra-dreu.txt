Mentor Milestones
The CRA-WP Distributed Research Experiences for Undergraduates (DREU) program offers a unique opportunity for undergraduate students to immerse themselves in computing research and gain firsthand experience working with faculty mentors at institutions across the U.S. This 10-week summer program helps students explore what graduate school is like, while mentors support the next generation of researchers within the computing field.
Program Mission
CRA-WP DREU provides research experiences for undergraduate students early in their academic careers, supporting their interest in advanced study and careers in computing research, and education. The program connects students with mentors to develop research skills and gain valuable experience in the field.
DREU participants are encouraged to develop their skills, explore potential career paths, and form meaningful relationships with faculty and graduate students.
In 2026, DREU expands to include coordinated research opportunities in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Quantum Information Science (QIS) , building on the program’s distributed model with shared cohort experiences and specialized programming.
AI & Quantum Opportunities in DREU
In 2026, the CRA-WP DREU program expands to include coordinated research opportunities in two emerging areas: Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Quantum Information Science (QIS).
These opportunities build on the core DREU model—placing students in full-time, mentored research experiences—while adding a centrally coordinated national cohort experience that connects participants across institutions.
Artificial Intelligence (AI)
DREU opportunities in Artificial Intelligence combine immersive research placements with a shared cohort experience designed to support students exploring research in areas such as machine learning, trustworthy AI, and AI systems.
Quantum Information Science (QIS)
DREU opportunities in Quantum Information Science combine immersive research placements with a shared cohort experience designed to support students exploring research in areas such as quantum algorithms, systems, and hybrid quantum-classical approaches.
Additional opportunity:
Travel support to attend IEEE Quantum Week (September 13–18, 2026)
Engagement with the broader quantum research community
How this fits within DREU
All students—whether participating in AI, Quantum, or other DREU placements—engage in full-time research with faculty mentors.
AI and Quantum opportunities offer an additional layer of coordination and community through shared cohort experiences, while maintaining the core strengths of the DREU program.
Who Can Participate?
Who Can Participate?
Undergraduate Students:
Open to students from U.S. institutions (priority given to those in computer science, computer engineering, or related fields).
Open to U.S. citizens, permanent residents, and nationals.
Students do not need prior research experience to apply.
Faculty Mentors:
Eligible mentors come from a broad range of institutions, including PhD-granting universities, liberal arts colleges, and minority-serving institutions.
Mentors collaborate with students on research projects and help them build confidence and skills for future academic pursuits.
What is an undergraduate student?
An undergraduate student is a student who is enrolled in a degree program (part-time or full-time) leading to a baccalaureate or associate degree. Students who are transferring from one college or university to another and are enrolled at neither institution during the intervening summer may participate. High school graduates who have been accepted at an undergraduate institution but who have not yet started their undergraduate study are also eligible to participate. Students who have received their bachelor&#8217;s degrees and are no longer enrolled as undergraduates are generally not eligible to participate.
Program Benefits and Key Features
Program Benefits and Key Features
Stipend and Support:
Students earn $9,000 for 10 weeks of research.
Additional support covers relocation expenses and travel to an academic conference.
Collaborative Experience:
Participants work alongside faculty mentors and graduate students, gaining a deeper understanding of academic research and networking with the research community.
Opportunities to participate in coordinated, cohort-based research experiences in emerging areas such as AI and quantum computing.
What to Expect from CRA-WP DREU
What to Expect from CRA-WP DREU
This program provides:
A chance for students to explore computing research and discover if graduate school is the right path for them.
An opportunity for faculty mentors to develop future researchers and promote diversity in the field.
Valuable connections that students can leverage as they pursue graduate admissions, fellowships, and other career goals.
Some students will also participate in cohort-based experiences in AI or Quantum Information Science, connecting them with peers and programming across institutions.
Program History and Support
Program History and Support
CRA-WP DREU is led by the CRA-Widening Participation (CRA-WP) committee and funded by the U.S. National Science Foundation.
With nearly 30 years of experience, DREU has connected thousands of students with meaningful research opportunities.
